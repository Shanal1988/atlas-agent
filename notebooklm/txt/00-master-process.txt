# My Investing Process

> Extracted from my personal "Stock Analysis" Google Sheet — the process I have followed for the last 5 years.
> Influences: Buffett, Munger's Four Filters, Philip Fisher's 15 Points, Peter Lynch's six categories, Brian Feroldi's Quality Score / Anti-Fragile Score, and Motley Fool-style risk ratings.

---

## 1. Philosophy

Long-term, quality-growth investing in businesses that are:
- Simple to understand and inside my circle of competence
- Run by able, trustworthy, owner-minded management ("soul in the game")
- Protected by an identifiable, ideally widening, moat
- Capable of compounding sales, earnings and FCF for years
- Bought only at a price that affords a margin of safety

Investing is treated as a game of mistakes: avoid making them while looking for mistakes made by others (who's selling and why?).

---

## 2. Workflow (Pipeline)

1. **Idea capture** → log every new idea in the watchlist with source and a quick ComboScore
2. **Research** → filings, transcripts, competitors, industry reports (with defined pause points)
3. **Checklists & scoring** → Munger Four Filters → BMP → Fisher 15 → Qualitative 8 → Feroldi Quality Score → Anti-Fragile Score → Risk Rating → Vital Signs
4. **Valuation** → estimate intrinsic value *before* looking at market valuation; earning-yield veto
5. **Position sizing** → based on "crushability" (# of NO answers) and investment stage
6. **Monitoring** → dashboard tracks earnings dates, re-read dates, price vs. intrinsic value, % off 52-week high/low

## 5. Munger's Four Filters (first gate)

1. Do I understand the business?
2. Sustainable competitive advantages (long-term favourable economics)?
3. Able and trustworthy management?
4. Price that affords a margin of safety?

All four must eventually be YES; price is often the last "TBC".

---

## 6. BMP Checklist (Business / Management / Price) — score out of 5

**The Business**
- Does the company have a low market share? (small share = lots of room to grow)
- …of a large and growing market? (exponential growth potential)
- …with a clearly identifiable competitive advantage that will let it grow sales and profits for years to come?

**Management Quality**
- Do the managers think and act like owners? (how they spend money, handle acquisitions, buybacks, options)
- Do the managers understand what drives business value? (Buffett/Bezos-style economic-value thinking)

**Moat types referenced:** low-cost producer, switching costs, network effects, brand.

**BMP Scorecard**
| Score | Verdict |
|---|---|
| 4–5 / 5 | Could be a great long-term holding → proceed to price |
| 3 / 5 | Wait and watch — "NOs" could become "YESes" |
| 0–2 / 5 | Not a long-term prospect — reject |

**PRICE — "the veto question":** Can I arrive at a reasonable earnings yield (> 5%)?
If NO → wait for Mr. Market to give the opportunity.

## 7. Fisher's 15 Points (score out of 15 — feeds the dashboard)

Each question scored 0–1 (halves/quarters allowed):

1. Products/services with sufficient market potential for a sizable increase in sales for several years? *(competitive edge)*
2. Management determination to keep developing products/processes beyond current lines? *(innovation)*
3. How effective is R&D relative to size? *(compare R&D to competitors — bigger moat)*
4. Above-average sales organization? *(Glassdoor, customer reviews/feedback)*
5. Worthwhile profit margin? *(operating profit over several years)*
6. What is the company doing to maintain/improve margins? *(cost optimisation, economies of scale)*
7. Outstanding labour/personnel relations? *(Glassdoor, low churn, wages)*
8. Outstanding executive relations? *(exec pay in line with industry)*
9. Depth to management? *(must survive without one brilliant manager)*
10. Quality of cost analysis and accounting controls? *(check the CFO and their record)*
11. Industry-specific clues to how outstanding the company is vs. competition? *(industry reports)*
12. Short-range or long-range outlook on profits? *(prefer sacrificing short-term profit for long-term growth)*
13. Will growth require equity financing that dilutes existing holders?
14. Does management talk freely when things are good but "clam up" in trouble?
15. Management of unquestionable integrity?

---

## 8. Eight Qualitative Questions (per company)

1. Is the business simple to understand and run?
2. Has it grown sales and EPS consistently over the past 5–10 years? *(record the 5-yr CAGRs)*
3. Will the company be around and profitably better in 10 years?
4. How has it performed on **Buffett's $1 test**? *(value created per $1 retained)*
5. Does it have a sustainable moat? Check 8 factors: pricing power, high gross margins, entry barriers, intangible assets, switching costs, network effect, cost advantage, efficient scale → conclude None / Narrow / Wide
6. How good is management given the hand it has been dealt? *(capital allocation, ROE > 20%, no misgovernance)*
7. Does it require heavy capex / working capital to grow?
8. Does it generate more cash than it consumes? *(positive and growing FCF)*

| Item | Penalty |
|---|---|

## 10. Anti-Fragile Score (−7 to 17)

**1. Barbell method**
- Mission statement: simple, inspirational, optionable (−1 to +2)
- Moat: network effect, switching costs, low-cost, intangibles (0 to +2 each, max 8)
- Optionality (+1 / +2 / +3)

**2. Financial fortitude**
- Cash, debt, free cash flow (−1 / 0 / +1)
- Concentration (0 to −3)

**3. Skin in the game**
- Glassdoor (+1 if ≥ 4.0, 0 if 3.5–4.0, −1 if < 3.5)
- Founder involved (+1)
- Ownership (−1 if < 4%, 0 if 4–8%, +1 if > 8%)

**Interpretation:** ignore below 7 · 7–12 investable · 12+ fantastic.

## 11. Risk Rating ("Crushability") — 25 YES/NO questions

Count the number of **NO** answers; fewer NOs = harder to crush = bigger position.

| # of "NO" answers | Crushable like a… | My max position |
|---|---|---|
| 0–5 | Diamond | 6–10% |
| 6–8 | Marble | 3–5% |
| 9–11 | Jawbreaker | 1–2% |
| 12–14 | Coconut | <1% |
| 15–18 | Glass bottle | 0 |
| 19–25 | Egg | 0 |

**About the company:** profitable last 12 months & last quarter · FCF-positive last 12 months & last quarter · recognisable brand · diversified buyer base (no customer > 20%) · positive word of mouth / fans.

**About the financials:** sales growth 10–40% over last 3 years (YES; >41% counts as NO — too hot) · can operate 3 years without external funds · high disclosure standard (SEC guidelines) · transparency (financials & insider ownership easy to find) · well-managed (ROE ≥ 15% most recent FY).

**Competition (subjective):** underdog (no direct competitor with greater resources) · goliath (free of disruptive upstarts) · moat (high entry barriers / SWOT / threat from direct competitors).

**Stock:** market cap > $500M · beta < 1.3 (past 12 months) · positive P/E < 30.

**Management:** key insider owns > 5% · top 3 CXOs have combined leadership > 15 years.

**Service-specific:** Stock Advisor-style (quality business, proven management, stalwart balance sheet; conscious capitalism) — or Rule Breaker-style (≥ 4 of the 6 Rule Breaker traits; can withstand a binary outcome).

**Other:** fraud-free in statements/deeds · "Do I want to know more about this company?" · 2 company-specific risk questions · bulletproof/antifragile to macro, inflation, natural events.

## 12. Ten Vital Signs (score 0 / 0.5 / 1 each; total out of 10)

1. **Strong balance sheet, preferably net cash?** — net cash vs net debt; debt trend; strategic vs necessity debt; cyclical + capital-intensive combo; operating + financial leverage; cost/maturities/fixed-vs-variable of debt; pensions & leases; credit rating; health ratios (net debt/FCF, interest coverage, D/E, net cash/market cap, goodwill/assets); ultimate stress test: how long can cash cover total annual expenses at zero revenue?
2. **Organic revenue growth powered by a large market / long-term tailwinds?** — TAM and growth, secular themes, product relevance and demand, customer & product obsession, investment in innovation/R&D/talent, reinvestment opportunities at high returns, go-to-market strategy.
3. **Rising or stable margins (gross and NOPAT)?** — what drives the change: pricing power, scale/operating leverage, mix shift, acquisitions, cost programs, tax; fixed vs variable cost model; mission-critical yet small % of customer's costs; profitable growth vs growth-at-any-cost culture.
4. **High/increasing ROIC, growing earnings and FCF?** — unique model, barriers to entry & success, share leader with growing share, best product & service, integrated into customer's business, fundamental to our way of life, sources of advantage (culture, adaptability, brand, switching costs, scale, network effects); moat being protected and new moats built; DuPont analysis; management comp & capital allocation based on ROIC/FCF.
5. **Exceptional CEO and leadership team?** — founder, high inside ownership, long-term over short-term EPS, someone I'd work for, compassionate/capable/candid/committed, soul in the game, healthy enduring culture, strong No. 2.
6. **Recurring revenue and/or pricing power?** — sources (contracts, subscriptions, consumables, habits, mission-critical), frequency & size of price increases, durability.
7. **Medium (or lower) risk profile?** — debt, mismanagement, deteriorating vitals, commodity exposure, cyclicality & capital intensity, business-model risk, low entry barriers, industry headwinds, disruption/obsolescence, customer/supplier concentration, supply-chain reliance, earnings-quality red flags, poor service, toxic culture, data privacy, opacity, no succession plan, short-termism, brand/reputation risk, burdensome regulation.
8. **Executing well (strong business momentum)?** — organic growth maintained/accelerating, taking share, winning contracts, price increases with strong demand, best product/workplace recognition, talent, rising margins & ROIC, successful acquisition integration.
9. **Mission beyond maximizing profits?** — solving an important problem, actions align with mission, stakeholder-first approach, shared value creation and win-win outcomes.
10. **Multi-bagger potential?** — committed to innovation and adaptability, future-proofing, product changes how we work/live, reflects the world of the future, saves customers time or money, embedded optionality (net cash + strong FCF, cash redirected into fast-growing adjacencies or new markets, first mover in an emerging frontier).

Plus two closing questions:
- Is the stock selling at a sane valuation?
- What is the opportunity cost of my capital? (Is this better than buying more of what I already own?)

## 15. Valuation Toolkit

### Ratio thresholds

| Ratio | Formula | Bad | Good |
|---|---|---|---|
| P/E | Price / EPS | Above industry average | Below industry average |
| PEG | P/E / net income growth rate | > 1 | < 1 (0.7 cut-off — anything lower merits further investigation) |
| P/B | Market cap / (assets − liabilities) | > industry | < 1.5 and below industry |
| ROE | Net profit / shareholder equity | < 20% | > 20% |
| Earnings growth | | < 20% | > 20% |
| Debt/Equity | | > 40% | < 40% |
| Debt coverage | Debt covered by operating cash flow | < 20% covered | ≥ 20% covered |
| Interest coverage | EBIT / interest | < 3x | ≥ 3x |
| Capex | % of net earnings | | < 25% very good, < 50% acceptable |
| P/S | | | Use for growth companies |
| ROCE | EBIT / (total assets − current liabilities) | | |

### ROIC
- ROIC = NOPAT / invested capital, where NOPAT = operating profit × (1 − effective tax rate)
- Invested capital = equity + debt + capital lease obligations
- Benchmark for value creation: **~2 percentage points above cost of capital**
- Do a DuPont analysis of the drivers

### Owner's Earnings (Buffett)
Reported earnings + depreciation + depletion + amortisation + non-cash items ± deferred taxes − capex (needed to maintain long-term competitive position).
Equivalently: FCF ± change in working capital.

**Working capital note:** if change in working capital is negative, the company needs more capital to grow (cash consumed, FCF reduced). If positive, it can grow with less capital (payments delayed / cash upfront).

### FCF investor's 10 metrics
FCF growth · FCF per share growth · FCF margin (FCF/sales) · FCF conversion (FCF/net income) · FCF ROIC · Net debt/FCF · Dividends/FCF · Buybacks/FCF · (Dividends+Buybacks)/FCF · FCF/EV

### Quality shorthand
- Gross margin > 40% (scalability) · Net margin > 20% · consistent earnings · low debt · growing earnings · reducing capex
- Closer the equity (book value) is to market price, the safer the investment

### Identifying the moat
Cost advantage · size/scale advantage · high switching costs · intangibles (patents, brand, licenses).
Ask: what are the revenue sources? Which is the cash cow? What industry? Who are the competitors? What makes it stand out?

## 16. Research Process (per company)

**Steps (logged with dates):**
1. Read 10-Qs, 10-Ks, proxies and other filings in **reverse chronological order**
2. Press releases and earnings calls/transcripts
3. Other public information — court documents, real-estate records, etc.
4. Industry publications
5. Third-party analysts (sell-side research)
6. Research the company's competitors
7. **Estimate valuation before looking at market valuation** — what would a rational long-term private buyer pay in cash today for the entire business?

**Requirements before investing:**
- Large, well-understood margin of safety
- Reinvestment opportunities for capital in the business
- Quality, ownership stake and shareholder-orientation of management
- Ability to bear pain — both the company's and my own

**Pause points:**
- **Pause 1** — Are the business and its securities able to be understood and valued?
- **Pause 2** — Go back through all financial disclosure for information/context missed the first time (patterns/trends, level and quality of disclosure)
- **Pause 3 — final checks** — What can go wrong? Do a **pre-mortem** (view from 1/3/5 years in the future where the idea failed horribly; seek outside input). How can capital be permanently impaired? What are the probabilities — are the odds heavily in my favor? Time horizon? How attractive vs. my best current investment?

**Ideas to consider throughout:**
1. Separate the business from the balance sheet — capitalization, sustainability, liquidation/reproduction value, hidden assets/liabilities (excess cash, real estate, LIFO, pensions, litigation)
2. Separate the business from the cash flows — normalized owner's earnings, earnings yield (EBIT/TEV), ROIC (EBIT/(WC+fixed assets)), capex requirements vs inflation/depreciation
3. Competitive situation & management — what could kill the business, incremental margins, capital allocation, end-market trajectory, susceptibility to rapid technological change
4. Other — market perceptions, alignment of interests, is this worth a punch on my punch card?
5. Psychological factors — psychology of misjudgment, common biases
6. Where are we in the cycle? — economic cycle, risk-asset cycle, industry cycle

**Final checks:**
- Who's selling? Why? Who's making the mistake here — buyer or seller?
- Pre-mortem with outside input

## 17. Per-Company Analysis Template

Each company under serious research gets its own tab containing:

1. **Position log** — date, buy price, Feroldi Quality Score, Anti-Fragile score, recurring revenue Y/N, profitable Y/N, FCF-positive Y/N, 5-yr beat SPY?, 5-yr revenue/earnings growth, P/S, trailing/forward P/E, P/FCF, PEG, forward PEG, market cap, dividend yield, EV — re-logged at each purchase/review date
2. **Checklists** — Munger Four Filters, BMP, Fisher 15, Feroldi Quality Score (with reasons per line), Anti-Fragile Score
3. **Financial history** — 10 years of income statement with YoY growth and margins (revenue, gross profit/margin, opex, operating income/margin, net income), plus balance-sheet and cash-flow items
4. **KPIs specific to the business** — e.g. MAU/ARPU (Spotify/Pinterest), orders/GTV (Deliveroo), nights booked/GBV by region (Airbnb), users/premium subs, revenue diversification by segment
5. **Industry research** — TAM, growth forecasts, key takeaways, competitor ratio comparisons (P/E, PEG, P/B, ROE vs industry and market)
6. **Intrinsic value estimates** — own estimate plus cross-checks (e.g. SimplyWallSt, BuffettBooks), with margin of safety applied

## 18. Monitoring & Maintenance

- Update dashboard prices, % off 52W high/low, P/E, market cap regularly
- Track **earnings release dates**; re-read/re-score after results ("Start Reading" / "Last Read" dates)
- Re-estimate intrinsic value periodically and date-stamp it
- Re-run Feroldi score at later dates and compare (score history is kept per company)
- Watchlist tracks price performance since idea was added — a feedback loop on idea sources and my own judgment
